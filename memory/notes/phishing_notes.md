# phishing notes

**Phishing Attacks**

A phishing attack is a type of social engineering attack that tricks users into revealing sensitive information, such as passwords, credit card numbers, or personal data, through deceptive emails, messages, or websites.

**How Phishing Works:**

1. **Spoofing**: Attackers create fake emails, messages, or websites that appear to be from a trusted source.
2. **Deception**: The attacker's goal is to trick the user into believing they are communicating with a legitimate organization.
3. **Revealing Sensitive Info**: Once the user clicks on the link or provides sensitive information, it is harvested by the attacker.

**Types of Phishing Attacks:**

1. **Email Phishing**: Scammers send fake emails that appear to be from a trusted source, such as a bank or online retailer.
2. **Spear Phishing**: Attackers target specific individuals with tailored phishing messages.
3. **Whaling**: Sophisticated phishing attacks targeting high-level executives or decision-makers.

**Prevention:**

1. **Verify Sources**: Always verify the authenticity of emails, messages, or websites before responding or providing sensitive information.
2. **Be Cautious**: Be wary of spelling mistakes, grammatical errors, and suspicious links or attachments.
3. **Use Anti-Phishing Tools**: Utilize email clients with built-in phishing protection and use reputable antivirus software to block malicious websites.

**Buffer Overflow in Phishing:**

In some cases, attackers may exploit buffer overflow vulnerabilities in the programming languages used by websites to send phishing emails. This allows them to inject malicious code that can lead to further attacks.
